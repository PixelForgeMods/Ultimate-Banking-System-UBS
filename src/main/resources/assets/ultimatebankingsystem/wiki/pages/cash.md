# Cash and wallets

> Physical tender, cash storage and deposits.

## Two kinds of money

Your **digital balance** lives in a UBS account. Bills and coins are **physical items** you can carry, store, drop or lose. [See every denomination](wiki:currency) and [learn about money straps](wiki:money-stacks).

ATMs withdraw whole-dollar bills. Supported bill and coin items can be deposited if your inventory contains an exact combination for the requested amount. Bank tellers can dispense bills and coins. A transfer or card payment moves digital funds without creating cash items.

## Wallets and briefcases

A wallet stores physical cash. A money briefcase stores bundled money stacks and up to **six gold or silver bars**. Place it, **sneak-right-click** to open it, then use a held stack or bar on the open case to add it. Empty-hand right-click withdraws the most recently stored item. It may be a bar rather than a stack.

Breaking the briefcase preserves its contents in the dropped briefcase item. Bars are valuables, not bill or coin denominations.

## Notes and cheques are different

Bank notes and cheques are account-backed documents, not loose legal-tender bills. They are not counted as a handful of dollar bills when a cash deposit asks for physical tender.
