# Desktop notification research

## Motion and layout references

- Windows 11 displays a temporary banner above the lower-right taskbar. The notification card identifies its app, then places the title and message ahead of any image, progress, or actions. The video is a supplemental reference for the banner's slide and the Microsoft Learn article supplies examples of card content. [Windows 11 toast walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5otbwX4o_88), [Microsoft Learn app notification UX](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/notifications/app-notifications/app-notifications-ux-guidance), [Windows 11 screenshot-related toast example](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2024/01/18/announcing-windows-11-insider-preview-build-23619-dev-channel/)
- Notification Center enters from the right edge and follows the finger for both touch opening and dismissal. Microsoft's animated example shows the panel with notification history and calendar. [Official Notification Center GIF](https://blogs.windows.com/wp-content/uploads/prod/sites/44/2022/02/Invoking-Notifications.gif), [Windows Insider Build 22557](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2022/02/16/announcing-windows-11-insider-preview-build-22557/)
- Windows opens the center by clicking the clock or bell, pressing Win+N, or swiping from the right edge on touch devices. The motion references show the direction and placement. Microsoft does not specify the duration or easing for the native keyboard and mouse animation in these sources. [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/notifications-and-do-not-disturb-in-windows)
- Microsoft documents five seconds as the default banner dwell time, with an accessibility setting to extend it. WinUI guidance separately gives 167 and 250 milliseconds as standard durations for fast and normal control transitions. Those are useful reference values for an in-game animation. [Microsoft accessibility guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/accessibility/windows/make-windows-easier-to-hear), [WinUI timing and easing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/motion/timing-and-easing)

## Delivery and interaction

- Banner visibility and history are independent delivery choices. A regular banner times out and remains available in history when the sender's Notification Center option is enabled. Clicking the card can open the source app or related content. Users can dismiss one notification, dismiss a sender's group, clear all, expand a card, or manage the sender through its menu. [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/notifications-and-do-not-disturb-in-windows), [Microsoft Learn app notification UX](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/notifications/app-notifications/app-notifications-ux-guidance)
- Apps supply the available actions and content. Payloads support a source icon, title, body, attribution, timestamp, optional inline or hero image, contextual buttons, text input, and determinate or indeterminate progress. An ongoing operation can update the same notification as progress changes. [Microsoft Learn app notification content](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/notifications/app-notifications/app-notifications-content)
- A capture completion can present the screenshot with an action to open it for markup. The underlying screenshot and clipboard operation runs independently of banner delivery. [Microsoft Snipping Tool](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2021/08/12/first-set-of-windows-app-updates-rolling-out-to-windows-insiders-for-windows-11/), [Microsoft Snipping Tool draw and hold](https://blogs.windows.com/windows-insider/2025/03/13/snipping-tool-and-notepad-app-updates-begin-rolling-out-to-windows-insiders/)

## Settings, priority, and Focus

- Windows exposes global notification and sound controls, lock-screen visibility, and per-app controls for enablement, banners, history, sound, and hiding content on the lock screen. Top, High, and Normal determine order in Notification Center. Only one app can be Top. The priority list for bypassing Do Not Disturb is a separate setting with entries for calls, reminders, and chosen apps. [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/notifications-and-do-not-disturb-in-windows)
- Do Not Disturb sends ordinary notifications directly to history when enabled. Users can toggle it from the center or use automatic rules for times and system states such as fullscreen use. [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/notifications-and-do-not-disturb-in-windows)
- Focus sessions start from the notification and calendar flyout, Settings, or the Clock app. A session can activate Do Not Disturb, hide badges or taskbar flashing, and show a timer. Ending the session restores pre-session Do Not Disturb behavior. [Microsoft Focus documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/experience/focus-stay-on-task-without-distractions-in-windows)
